BTI · Your S01–S07 route

Bucharest University of Economic Studies · CSIE · Year 1, semester 1 · 7 seminars × 90 minutes

We begin with values, representations and rules. In the final three seminars we use these ideas in an existing software project: change, investigate, test and publish. Git records versions while GitHub helps us retrieve the published version.

Using the materials

  1. To read the lessons and try the application, download the whole repository through Code → Download ZIP and extract every file and folder. Open START.html from the extracted folder, then choose your language.
  2. For Git work in S05–S07, create your own repository once through Use this template → Create a new repository on the shared source announced in the course. Clone your own repository URL in VS Code and open START.html from that clone.
  3. Choose a seminar below. Open the interactive lesson for guided work or the reading version for explanations on one page. Keep START.html and the LESSONS, GUIDES and PROJECT folders together.

Already started S05? Continue in the same personal repository throughout S05–S07. Do not replace it or create another template-derived repository to obtain these materials. Pulling from your own repository retrieves changes published there; it does not automatically import later template changes. You can read the new lessons from a separate copy of the complete archive while keeping your project and its history.

Before the seminar

Consult the lesson opening and the preparation required for the activity. Before S05, check Git, VS Code, your browser, your own GitHub account and access to the Moodle course by following the lesson steps.

During the seminar

Follow the instructions for your group. Make a prediction before calculating or running the program, compare expected and observed results and explain differences. In S01–S04 the core is separate from supplements.

After the seminar

Revisit an explanation that remains unclear. In S05–S07 retain the project’s resumption notes, commits and checks you actually performed. They help you continue at the next meeting.

The lessons and application open locally in a browser. GitHub displays HTML files as source; open them from the extracted or cloned folder. GitHub and Moodle require online access. Local results are not automatically submitted to Moodle.

The seven seminars

Each seminar lasts 90 minutes. Follow the core route during class and revisit fuller explanations and supplements when needed. Assessment requirements and any required supplements are those announced in the course.

S01 · Number bases and representations

What you learn
Distinguish a value from the way it is written.
What you do
Convert between decimal, binary and hexadecimal, practise binary addition and subtraction and check the limit of an unsigned byte.
What you should be able to explain
Justify a conversion by reversing it and explain why 255 fits in eight bits while 256 does not.
What to retain
Declare the base and width. Check the value through an independent method.

S02 · Signed numbers, fixed point and floating point

What you learn
Interpret bits under a declared format.
What you do
Compare signed representations, distinguish overflow from carry, encode fixed-point values and reconstruct a normal binary32 number.
What you should be able to explain
Explain why the same bit pattern can represent different values under different formats and check the range and rounding.
What to retain
Bits do not declare their own sign, scale or format.

17 core pages · 6 supplementary pages

Open the interactive lessonOpen the reading version

S03 · Parity, Hamming and CRC

What you learn
Check what a method can detect or correct and under which conditions.
What you do
Construct even parity, encode and check Hamming (7,4), calculate a teaching CRC and look for errors that can pass a check.
What you should be able to explain
Show a detected case and a relevant counterexample without attributing guarantees the check does not provide.
What to retain
Passing a check supports a limited conclusion. It does not itself prove data identity or authenticity.

S04 · Boolean logic and a short architecture introduction

What you learn
Turn a rule into results you can check.
What you do
Connect a statement to an expression, truth table and circuit. Check equivalences and trace the instructions of a teaching machine.
What you should be able to explain
Reject an equivalence with a counterexample and track registers separately from modified memory.
What to retain
Track the condition definitions and program state explicitly.

S05 · Your first tested and published change

What you learn
Take over an existing project and record your first change in its history.
What you do
Create your own template-derived repository, clone it in VS Code and complete a byte’s hexadecimal display using exactly two uppercase characters. Test, read the differences and stage, commit and push.
What you should be able to explain
Identify where the change exists after each operation and find the published commit on GitHub.
What to retain
Saving, staging, committing and pushing have different effects.

S06 · A hypothesis, a test and an explained fix

What you learn
Investigate a problem before choosing the fix.
What you do
Reproduce an incorrect signed interpretation, write your hypothesis, consult Gemini or the prepared example and test a claim. Describe the problem in an issue, keep the fix on a separate branch and integrate it through a pull request and merge, then update the clone with pull.
What you should be able to explain
Explain which observation distinguished two explanations and which copy contains the integrated fix.
What to retain
A useful test can refute your hypothesis. A persuasive explanation needs checking.

S07 · An individual task and a verified version

What you learn
Complete your assigned requirement and check the version you submit.
What you do
Individually implement the separate parity bit in your assigned mode, retain previously checked behaviour and publish. From a fresh clone check the branch, version identifier and results, then complete the final Moodle record.
What you should be able to explain

Investigation with Gemini

Begin with your own prediction or hypothesis. Choose a precise claim from the explanation, then check it through calculation, counting or a project test. Complete prompts and examples are in the lessons.

S06 requires investigation of an assisted explanation. Use Gemini if access is available or the prepared teaching alternative in the lesson. Both require the same independent check, with no penalty for choosing the alternative. Consultation is optional in S05 and S07.

No paid subscription or new account is needed to complete the activity through the prepared alternative. Record the source and conclusion honestly. Prompts need the requirement, hypothesis and relevant results, rather than passwords, tokens or personal data.

  1. Your own prediction or hypothesis
  2. Claim from Gemini or the prepared example
  3. A justified expected result
  4. Independent calculation or test
  5. Conclusion and limits

In the quizzes work individually without AI or help from classmates. Only the shared reference sheet announced in the course is permitted. Close the extended lesson and working tools. Follow the window and conditions displayed in Moodle.

In S07 submit the ten-field record as online text: repository, version, assigned mode, instructions, tests, investigation and reproduction. The lesson provides the complete structure. Describe actual blockages and results. Saving and checking Moodle’s displayed status confirm submission.

The quiz checks understanding of the described situations. Its mark does not itself prove that a repository was published or a result reproduced. Keep assessment answers distinct from the checks performed in the project.

The final ten-field record is submitted as online text and has no separate grade. Submission, completeness or approval of the record is not a condition for access to Q07. Follow the quiz and submission windows announced in the Moodle course.

What to retain when the details fade

You can look up formulas, menus and commands again. To resume a project, retain these questions:

  • What does the problem require and which convention am I using?
  • What result do I expect and why?
  • What did I actually observe?
  • Which test could refute my explanation?
  • What did I change, in which version and where is it published?
  • What can someone else conclude from the check and how can they reproduce the result?
