One project, three seminars

Take over a small project, investigate a defect and leave a published version that can be reproduced.

Why we are doing this

Software work often begins with a project somebody else has already started. You need to understand what it does, change a small part and leave a version that can be found and checked later. We will practise that sequence with Bit & Byte Lab, a small browser application about eight-bit data.

The interface and most functions are provided. Your three changes are limited: display hexadecimal notation, repair a signed interpretation error and add one parity rule. The substantial work is to predict a result, check it and explain why your change meets the requirement.

The lasting habit: identify the requirement → predict an observable result → test a change → preserve the reason → publish an identifiable version. The commands may be forgotten; this method can be used again in another project.

How the one point is earned

The three Moodle quizzes are automatically marked. Their internal maxima are 20, 30 and 50; the total is divided by 100, so the block contributes at most one real seminar point. For example, 16 + 24 + 40 gives 0.80 real points. A missed quiz does not increase another quiz’s weight.

Each quiz is individual, without AI or help from classmates. The shared reference is allowed. The questions check whether you understand the representations, Git states and evidence behind a result. Completing the exercises prepares you to answer them.

The final online-text assignment is compulsory and has no separate grade. It collects your repository URL, full final commit identifier, variant, launch instructions, three test results, one checked AI claim, fresh-clone result and source template. No PDF, screenshots or complete chat transcript are required.

A submitted record is not automatic proof of authorship or a fully correct implementation. The quiz is an understanding check, not an automatic audit of every repository. If Moodle uses submission completion to open Q07, that condition checks that the record exists.

When you may use Gemini

S05: Gemini is optional for clarification. S06: write your own hypothesis first, then investigate a response from Gemini or the prepared fallback if access is unavailable. S07: clarification is allowed during practice; the assigned implementation remains individual. AI is not allowed during any quiz.

We will use the same sequence: your guess → one precise AI claim → a test with a predicted result → the observed result → your conclusion. An initial guess that turns out to be wrong is acceptable: keep it and explain how the evidence changed your view. An access problem or free-use limit does not require a paid subscription.

Your first action

  1. Open Before S05 and check tools and account access on the computer you will use.
  2. At S05, open the shared BBS-bit-byte-starter template. Create your own repository from it, then clone your own URL.
  3. Follow S05, S06 and S07 in order. Keep one repository throughout.

If the shared template cannot be opened or its template button is missing, tell us. A downloaded package is useful for looking at the files; it does not replace creating and cloning your own GitHub repository.
